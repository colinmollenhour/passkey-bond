The short version

Nothing to hand over.

We built Passkey.bond so there is nothing for us to lose, leak or sell. That isn't a policy. It's the design.

No account

There's no sign-up and no Passkey.bond password to steal. Install it and you're done.

No vault server

Your vault lives on your devices. When they sync, they pass sealed envelopes through relays, ours among them, that can't open them.

No secrets

The code is open source. Anyone can read exactly what it does, line by line.

A companion, not a replacement

Don't keep both keys on one ring.

Two-factor codes and passkeys exist so that one stolen secret isn't enough. Keep them in the same password manager as your passwords, and a single breach of that vault hands over both.

On record

In 2022, attackers stole encrypted copies of LastPass customers' vaults from its cloud storage. Copies like that can be attacked offline, for as long as the attackers like. A vault that never leaves your devices can't be taken in bulk.

Keep your password manager for passwords. Use Passkey.bond for the keys that keep them honest.

How sync works

Synced, never opened.

Your devices swap sealed envelopes through relays, rather like leaving a locked case at a left-luggage office. We run one of the offices, independent operators run the others, and none of them holds a key. Neither do we.

How it works

Set up in a minute. Then forget it's there.

  1. Install

    Add Passkey.bond to your browser. It's free, and there's nothing to sign up for.

  2. Sign in as usual

    When a site offers a passkey, Passkey.bond creates it and keeps it. Next time, one click signs you in.

  3. Add your phone

    Turn on sync and type your 12-word phrase into the phone app. Your devices now share one vault.

Questions

Asked, and answered.

Can Passkey.bond see my passkeys?

No. Without sync, nothing leaves your device. With sync on, your devices send encrypted messages through relays, including one we run, and only your devices hold the key to open them. Our relay sees the same sealed noise as everyone else's.

What if I lose my laptop or phone?

If sync is on, your other devices still have everything. Keep your 12-word sync phrase or a backup file somewhere safe, and you can restore onto a new device.

Without either, a lost device takes its vault with it. There's no "forgot password" link, because there's no account behind it.

Why is the extension free and the phone app paid?

The extension is free, full stop. The phone app costs $2.95 once, about the price of a cheap coffee, and that pays for building and maintaining apps for Android and iPhone. Pay once and it's yours: no subscription, no renewals.

Does it replace my password manager?

No, and that's deliberate. Keep your passwords in your password manager and your passkeys and 2FA codes in Passkey.bond. If either one is ever breached, the attacker has only half of what they need.

Do I need the phone app?

No. The browser extension works on its own. The phone app is for when you also want your passkeys and codes on your phone.

Is it safe to sync through public relays?

Yes. A relay only ever sees sealed, signed messages. It can't read them, and it can't change them without your devices noticing. If one relay goes down, your devices use the others. The details are here.

What happens if Passkey.bond goes away?

Your vault keeps working, because it never depended on us. The code is open source, and you can export everything in standard formats whenever you like.

I already use Fenko Vault. Can I switch?

Yes. Passkey.bond grew out of Fenko Vault. Import a Fenko Vault backup, or join your existing sync chain with its phrase, and everything comes across.
